# Scientific review deck workflow

## Choose the review archetype

### Methods review

Use when the central question is “how can this biological state be measured?” Organize by measurement target rather than instrument name.

Recommended sequence:

1. Research scope and definitions
2. Biological states or measurement targets
3. Method map
4. Core methods: principle, direct readout, limitations
5. Published application cases
6. Orthogonal methods and evidence hierarchy
7. Method-selection matrix
8. Practical workflow for the user's research context
9. Quality control and interpretation boundaries
10. Summary and recommended minimum evidence chain

### Mechanism review

Use when the central question is “how does a process work?” Organize by causal sequence, competing models, and decisive evidence.

Recommended sequence:

1. Biological problem
2. Canonical mechanism
3. Unresolved question or competing models
4. Evidence supporting each model
5. Representative experiments
6. Context dependence and limitations
7. Working model and testable predictions

### Field or disease review

Use when the central question is “what is known, what remains uncertain, and why does it matter?” Organize by concepts and evidence strength, not chronology alone.

## Build the content matrix

For each proposed slide, write internally:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Purpose | What should the audience understand? |
| Claim | What is the single defensible takeaway? |
| Evidence | Which figure, table, or citation supports it? |
| Boundary | What cannot be concluded? |
| Transition | Why does the next slide follow? |

Delete slides whose purpose duplicates another slide.

## Use an evidence hierarchy

Keep these levels distinct:

1. Probe or assay signal
2. Localization, conformation, or biochemical fractionation
3. Target identity
4. Orthogonal validation
5. Perturbation, rescue, or causal evidence

Write the conclusion at the level actually supported. A pathway marker is evidence of a response, not automatically evidence of the structural lesion that triggered it.

## Handle published cases

Each case should answer four questions:

- What biological material was tested?
- What perturbation or comparison was made?
- What was measured directly?
- What conclusion is justified, and what remains unproven?

Use one or two representative panels rather than an unreadable full multi-panel figure. Keep the original panel labels and place the full citation in notes.

## Control scope

- For a 10–15 minute review, usually target 10–15 slides.
- For a 15–25 minute review, usually target 14–22 slides.
- Allocate more time to synthesis and method choice than to enumerating every published technique.
- Put optional depth in backup slides when requested.
