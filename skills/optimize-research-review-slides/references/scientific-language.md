# Scientific language and terminology

## Chinese scientific style

Use direct subject–predicate sentences. Prefer “结果提示”“该方法检测”“这一信号受……影响” over promotional or absolute wording.

Use:

- “支持” when evidence is consistent with a conclusion;
- “提示” when the result is indirect or preliminary;
- “显示” for direct observations;
- “不能单独证明” to state an interpretation boundary;
- “需要结合……验证” when orthogonal evidence is required.

Avoid:

- “全面揭示”“强力赋能”“革命性”“至关重要” without a precise reason;
- long noun chains and article-title-style slide headings;
- repeated three-part slogans, vague “研究表明,” and excessive transition phrases;
- treating correlation, colocalization, or marker induction as causation.

## Slide titles

Prefer biological object + measurable property, method + direct target, a question answered by the slide, or a concise supported conclusion.

Examples:

- “TPE-MI检测未折叠蛋白质的原理”
- “Filter-trap与SDD-AGE检测大尺寸聚集体”
- “应激标志物不能替代聚集证据”

Avoid titles containing multiple subordinate clauses or workflow commentary.

## Abbreviations and terms

At first talk-track occurrence, write:

```text
中文名称（English full name，ABBR）
```

For named probes whose acronym is not expanded by the original paper, do not invent an expansion. State the published probe name and its functional class.

Place a concise visible definition in a footer, side margin, or other genuine blank area. Repeat the full definition naturally in speaker notes. Do not add a glossary if it makes the main content unreadable; instead move the first use to the dedicated method slide.

Common exemptions may include Western blot, PCR, FACS, DNA, RNA, and terms explicitly exempted by the user. Audience familiarity determines the final choice.

## Speaker notes

Write notes for spoken delivery, not as a duplicate of slide text. A good note:

1. names the object or method;
2. explains the visual or comparison;
3. states the justified conclusion;
4. states a limitation when relevant;
5. transitions to the next question.

Keep references in a separate `[Sources]` block so the script remains readable.
